Why You Need This

The problem

You're a founder. You know LinkedIn matters. Every advisor, investor, and recruiter has told you. You've read the stats — founders who post regularly get more inbound leads, more talent, more trust.

But every week, writing a LinkedIn post falls to the bottom of your list. You're running a company. You're in meetings. You're putting out fires. By the time you have a free hour, the last thing you want to do is stare at a blank text box and try to sound smart.

So your LinkedIn sits there, dead. Your last post was 3 months ago. It was about hiring. It got 12 likes. Meanwhile, your competitor posts every Tuesday and Thursday and their inbox is full of inbound leads.

The fix

I write your posts for you. Not generic "thought leadership" that sounds like every other founder. Posts in your voice — your opinions, your stories, your industry takes. The kind of posts that make people stop scrolling and think "finally, someone saying something real."

You spend 15 minutes a week reviewing. I spend the hours writing. Your LinkedIn becomes the asset it should be — without it eating your time.

What I Actually Do

The work, plainly:

  • Learn your voice — I do a 45-minute call with you. I ask what you believe, what frustrates you, what you'd say if nobody was judging. I write down your phrases, your humour, your tone. Then I write in that voice — not mine.
  • Write your posts — 8 posts per month. Each one starts with a hook that stops the scroll, says something worth reading, and ends with something that gets people talking. 150-300 words. Short, punchy, human.
  • You review and approve — Every post goes in a shared doc. You read it. If it doesn't sound like you, tell me what's off. I fix it. If it sounds like you, you post it. You always have final say.
  • Track and adjust — I watch which posts get engagement and which don't. I double down on what's working. I drop what's not. Your content gets sharper every month.
  • Find your topics — I help you figure out what to post about. Your industry takes, your founder journey, your hiring philosophy, your contrarian opinions. The stuff you'd talk about over coffee but never think to write down.

How It Works

1

We talk for 45 minutes

Google Meet, no pressure. I ask you questions. You talk. I listen and take notes. I'm mining for your voice — your phrasing, your opinions, your humour, your anger points. This call is the foundation. Everything I write comes from this.

2

I write your first 4 posts — free

I draft them in a Google Doc using your voice. You read them. If they don't sound like you, tell me what's wrong. I fix them. If they sound like you, you post the first one. No charge for these. If you don't like them, you walk away. No risk.

3

You post. I watch. I adjust.

You publish the posts on your schedule. I track engagement — likes, comments, shares. I see which topics land and which fall flat. I use that data to make the next batch better.

4

Ongoing: 8 posts per month

Every month: I write 8 posts. You review, approve, and post. 15 minutes of your time per week. Your LinkedIn stays active, your voice stays authentic, and you never stare at a blank screen again.

I almost killed a feature last week that 40% of our power users rely on.

Why? Because our dashboard showed low overall usage. Looked like a dead feature. Easy cut.

But I talked to 3 customers before making the call. Turns out those power users run their entire weekly reporting flow through it. Kill the feature, kill their workflow.

Dashboards tell you what. They never tell you why. Only conversations do.

I almost made a $200K churn decision based on a graph. A 15-minute call saved it.

Talk to your customers. The data can wait.

A client told me last month: "We love your work, but we went with the cheaper agency."

I said: "I understand. Can I ask — what made them cheaper?"

They paused. "They didn't ask about our goals. They just sent a quote."

And there it is. The cheaper option isn't cheaper. It's less work. Less thinking. Less questioning. Less friction.

I'd rather lose a pitch because I asked too many questions than win one because I asked none.

The right client doesn't want less friction. They want better outcomes. And better outcomes come from uncomfortable questions.

We lost that client. But the ones who stay? They stay because we ask the hard things.

Nobody talks about the boring part of wealth building.

Everyone posts about their portfolio returns. Nobody posts about the 3am panic when the market drops 8% and you're wondering if you should sell everything.

Here's what I tell every client: the strategy isn't the hard part. The strategy is a spreadsheet. Any advisor can build one.

Turned down a $400K contract this week.

AK
Anna Kim
Co-founder, Kindred (HR Tech Startup)
Honest · Vulnerable · Building in public

I hired someone last year who was perfect on paper.

10 years of experience. Worked at companies I admire. Charismatic in interviews. Checked every box.

They lasted 4 months.

Not because they weren't capable. They were. But they couldn't work in ambiguity. Every time something shifted — and in a startup, everything shifts weekly — they froze. They needed process, structure, predictability.

We had none of those things. We still don't. We're building the plane while flying it.

I learned: hire for adaptability, not just ability. The best resume doesn't survive chaos. The best person does.

Cost me 4 months and a lot of awkward conversations to learn that. Sharing so it doesn't cost you the same.
